Most study apps are built around consuming information. Flashcards. Summaries. Re-reading the same notes over and over. But there's a problem with that. Passive review doesn't stick.

Cognitive science has known for decades that the best way to learn something is to teach it. When you have to explain a concept to someone else, you discover the gaps you didn't know you had. It's called the protégé effect.

The only problem? You can't always find a willing student.

So we built one.

This is StudyLens. You upload your notes — a PDF, a text file, anything — and you're dropped into a live conversation with Sam. Sam is an AI student who has never seen your topic before. He genuinely doesn't know. And it's your job to teach him.

Sam asks follow-up questions when your explanation is vague. He pushes back when something doesn't make sense. And every few turns, he repeats something back to you — slightly wrong — on purpose. If you catch the mistake, you get credit. If you miss it, it shows up later in your report.

The whole time, Sam is quietly tracking which concepts you explained confidently and which ones were shaky.

When you're done teaching, you get a personalised report. A grade. A breakdown of every concept, ranked weakest first. And Sam's own notes on exactly where your understanding started to slip.

Then comes the quiz. But this isn't a generic quiz. The wrong answers are built from your own misconceptions — the things you actually said during the session. You can't guess your way through it.

The whole app runs in a browser. No account needed. Just your notes, and Sam waiting to learn.

We built this because re-reading doesn't work. Teaching does. And now you have someone to teach.
